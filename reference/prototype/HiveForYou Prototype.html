A guide to Maya's IEP

Customer report · {{ curCreated }} · Written from 6 documents

In brief

Maya's reading has grown from 62 to 81 words a minute. Her goal is 90.

Her speech therapy time was halved in September. The documents don't say why.

Her annual review is due in March 2026, a good time to ask about both.

Understanding the situation

Maya is in fourth grade and has an IEP for reading and speech. Her plan was written in March 2025, after a speech-language evaluation and a psychoeducational evaluation. Since then, the school changed her speech services once and sent two progress reports.

What is going well

Maya's reading fluency has grown. When the IEP was written she read 62 words correct per minute. In February she read 81. Her goal for March 2026 is 90.

Her psychoeducational evaluation describes verbal reasoning as a relative strength.

What has changed

In September 2025, an amendment changed Maya's speech-language therapy from 60 minutes a week to 30. We didn't find a reason for the change in the amendment. Two months later, her progress report described her articulation goal as still progressing.

What deserves attention

Maya's psychoeducational evaluation recommends audio or read-aloud support for tests with long reading passages. Her IEP gives her extended time and a separate testing room, but doesn't mention audio support.

Work on Maya's math calculation goal seems to have started later in the year. In November it was listed as not yet introduced; by February it was an emerging skill.

Questions worth asking

You might bring these to the annual review.

  1. 1What information led to reducing speech therapy to 30 minutes a week?
  2. 2Was audio support for tests considered, as the evaluation recommends?
  3. 3What is the plan to help Maya reach 90 words a minute by March?
  4. 4When did instruction on the math calculation goal begin?

This report describes what Maya's documents say. It isn't legal or educational advice, and decisions about her plan rest with you and her IEP team.
